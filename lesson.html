BUSN 2100 · Unit 4 · Writing Emails

What the reader hears

Email carries your words. It does not carry your voice.

Part 1: the reader fills in the blanks.   Part 2: sometimes it is on purpose.

Part 1 · The reader fills in the blanks

Sam, the manager. 9:10 AM. Relaxed.
Hey, can you send me the Q2 numbers when you get a chance?
Daniel, the employee. 9:11 AM.
Oh no. This must be due today. I need to hurry.

Part 1 · The reader fills in the blanks

"When you get a chance" has no clock in it.

Email hides how urgent something is. So the reader guesses.

And people usually guess the stressful version.

Part 1

This is called

email urgency bias

Plain words: email leaves out the clock, so the reader invents one. Usually a fast one.

Part 1 · Try it

Sam types, still smiling.
Please correct this mistake.
Daniel reads it.
Why are they being so rude?

Same five words. Two different emails.

Part 1 · The reader fills in the blanks

Email has no voice and no face.

So the reader adds the emotion themselves.

A neutral email often lands as a rude one.

Part 1

This is called

negative tone bias

Plain words: when the tone is missing, the reader fills the gap. And the gap usually gets filled with something negative.

What the research found

Writers thought about 78 out of 100 readers would catch their tone.

Readers actually caught it in about 56 out of 100 messages.

56 out of 100 is close to a coin flip.

Kruger, Epley, Parker and Ng (2005), Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. Six studies on email and tone.

What the research found

Readers read email as more negative than the writer meant.

The gap grows when the reader is lower in rank than the writer.

And it grows when the workplace is already tense.

Byron (2008), Academy of Management Review. "Carrying too heavy a load? The communication and miscommunication of emotion by email."

Part 1 · The reader fills in the blanks

One warm sentence costs five seconds.

An email that is only correction gets read as cold. Add one line of context or thanks and the correction lands as help.

Good managers do this on purpose. Small affirmations, written down.

What the research found

So, just add a 🙂 ?

In work emails to someone new, a smiley did not make the writer seem warmer.

It made the writer seem less competent on first contact.

Glikson, Cheshin and van Kleef (2018), Social Psychological and Personality Science. "The Dark Side of a Smiley."

Daniel, phone in hand.
If I don't reply quickly, I will look lazy.

In many workplaces a fast reply is read as competence. Your silence is read as a message too.

Part 2 · Sometimes it is on purpose

So far the reader was adding tone that was not there.

Now the other case. The tone is there. On purpose. Hidden inside polite words.

Decode it · What does this line really say?

Per my last email, the file is attached.

plain words   "You did not read my email."

what it does   It makes you feel careless. Nobody said the word careless.

Decode it · What does this line really say?

Not sure if you saw my message from Monday.

plain words   "You ignored me."

what it does   It puts you on the defensive before you have typed a word.

Decode it · What does this line really say?

Going forward, let's make sure the numbers are checked.

plain words   "You made a mistake, and I want it on the record."

what it does   "Let's" sounds shared. The blame is not shared.

Decode it · What does this line really say?

I trust you'll use your best judgment.

plain words   "Do not ask me again."

what it does   It sounds like a compliment. If it goes wrong, it was your judgment.

Decode it · What does this line really say?

Let's aim to make this round smoother than last time.

plain words   "I remember last time. I want you to know I remember."

what it does   It brings up an old mistake without naming it. You cannot defend yourself against a thing nobody said.

Part 2

Polite words + a hidden complaint

= passive aggressive

Plain words: aggressive means it attacks. Passive means the attack hides inside politeness.

Part 2 · Why people write this way

Because it is deniable.

If you push back, they can say: "I only said thanks."

So the person who answers the hidden message out loud usually ends up looking like the problem.
