Reply, Call, or Escalate?BUSN 2100 · Unit 4 · a group case in six rounds
Start here

One boss. Four questions. A 3:00 PM deadline.

Escalate, plain words: take the problem to someone above your boss. Your group is Sarah today. You will read a real looking email thread, work out what is really being said, decide what to do, and still get the flyer printed on time.

How this works, step by step

1. Your professor says "Round 1." You click Round 1 at the top. Everyone in the room is on the same round.

2. You read the screen. Nobody talks out loud. All talking happens by typing in your Teams breakout room chat.

3. At the bottom of each round there is a pink box. It tells you exactly what to type, who types it, and how many minutes you have.

4. Every message you post starts with the round number, like R2: so your professor can find your work later.

5. When your professor says "Round 2," you click Round 2. You can always click back to re-read.

Two roles in every group

The Typist. One person per group. Picked in Round 1: the person whose first name comes first in the alphabet. No debate. The Typist posts the group's final answer at the end of each round, starting with R2 FINAL:, R3 FINAL:, and so on.

The Voices. Everyone else. You post your own thinking first in every round (your own R-line), then you agree, disagree, and add to each other by typing. The Typist posts own R-lines too.

Plain rule: first everyone posts their own answer. Then you discuss by typing. Then the Typist posts FINAL. Same shape every round.

Your three jobs today

1. Read the tone. What is the boss really saying underneath the polite words?

2. Choose the move. Reply by email, call, walk over, decide on your own and say so, copy another person, go to your boss's boss, or stay quiet.

3. Get the task done anyway. The flyer prints at 3:00 no matter how anyone feels.

How you earn your 10 points

After class your professor opens your breakout room chat and reads your own posts (the ones that start with R1 to R6). Five things, 2 points each. Here is what counts.

2 ptsYou showed up in the chat. You posted your own R-line in at least 4 of the 6 rounds. Example: a short "R1: polite but answered nothing" counts.
2 ptsYou translated. Your R2 post names one sentence and gives its plain words meaning in your own words, not copied from a teammate.
2 ptsYou voted with a reason from the case. Your R4 post has a letter and a fact from the case file. "C, because in April she never answered before the deadline" counts. "C, feels right" does not.
2 ptsYou touched the draft. Your R5 EDIT post adds, cuts, or fixes one thing in the group's email and says why. If you are the Typist, your R5 DRAFT and R5 FINAL count here.
2 ptsYou stayed professional. No mocking Ms. Glenn, no sarcasm, no piling on a groupmate. Disagreeing with reasons is exactly what we want.
Not in the Teams meeting today

No points for today's activity, same as every in class activity. Two lowest activity scores are dropped at the end of the semester.

Round 1 · Read it 5 minutes

The case file

Read everything. No chat yet. You need the whole picture before you can judge one email.

Who is who

Sarah is a program assistant in the College Outreach Office at a community college. It is her first full time job. She has been there 8 months.

Ms. Glenn is the Director of Outreach. Sarah's boss. 14 years at the college. In meetings most of the day. Likes to be kept in the loop. Has said more than once that she does not like "being pulled into small things."

Kevin Martinez works in Communications. Friendly. He approves anything that carries the college logo, so he sees every flyer before it prints.

Dr. Alvarez is the Dean of Students. Ms. Glenn's boss. Sarah has met her twice, both times in a hallway.

What happened last time

In April, Sarah's second month, the spring flyer went out with the wrong room number. 250 copies. Reprinted. Cost: $180 from the office budget.

Sarah had emailed Ms. Glenn to confirm the room. No reply came. Sarah used last year's room.

At the May staff meeting, Ms. Glenn mentioned "a reprint we could have avoided." She did not say Sarah's name. Everyone knew.

Today

Wednesday, 11:40 AM. The fall Parent Information Night flyer is done except for four details.

Campus Print Services needs the final PDF by 3:00 PM today to deliver 300 copies by Friday. The event is next Thursday evening. If the PDF is late, the next print slot is Monday, and the flyers go out two days before the event instead of a week before.

Sarah's email, 10:55 AM

To: Ms. Glenn  ·  Subject: Parent Night flyer: four quick confirmations before 3:00 print deadline

Hi Ms. Glenn,

The flyer is ready to go to print. Campus Print needs the final PDF by 3:00 today. Could you confirm four things?

  1. RSVP link. The web page goes live Friday. Print the link now, or print "RSVP at the Outreach Office" instead?
  2. Speaker title. The draft says "Dr. Reyes, Director of Community Programs." I think her title changed to Dean in June. Which one goes on the flyer?
  3. Childcare hours. Your email last week said 6:00 to 8:30 PM. The childcare contract says 6:00 to 8:00. Which is right?
  4. Spanish version. Print it in the same run, or post it online only? Same run adds two days to the vendor timeline.

If it is easier, I can call or swing by, whatever works for you. I just do not want to guess on details that might change.

Thank you,
Sarah

Ms. Glenn's reply, 11:38 AM

From: Ms. Glenn  ·  Subject: Re: Parent Night flyer: four quick confirmations before 3:00 print deadline

Hi Sarah,

Thanks for staying on top of the flyer. I'm glad you're thinking about these kinds of things. That shows initiative.

Let's aim to make this round smoother than last time. I trust you'll use your best judgment with the info we have. These kinds of small edits shouldn't hold us back from moving forward.

Please keep me posted once the final version goes out.

Best,
M. Glenn

Count the answers. Sarah asked four questions. How many did she get answered?

What to do · 5 minutes
  1. Minute 1 to 3. Everyone reads the case file alone. No typing yet.
  2. Minute 4. Click Show Ms. Glenn's reply. Read it. Count how many of the four questions she answered.
  3. Minute 5. Each person posts one message that starts with R1: and gives your first honest reaction in five words or fewer. Example: R1: polite, but she dodged everything.
  4. Also in minute 5: whoever has the first name that comes first in the alphabet posts "I am the Typist." Done.
Round 2 · Translate it 6 minutes

What is Ms. Glenn really saying?

Her reply is polite in every sentence. But some sentences say one thing on the surface and mean something else underneath. Find the three sentences that have a hidden meaning, and write what each one really means in plain words.

From: Ms. Glenn  ·  11:38 AM

Hi Sarah,

Thanks for staying on top of the flyer. I'm glad you're thinking about these kinds of things. That shows initiative.

Let's aim to make this round smoother than last time. I trust you'll use your best judgment with the info we have. These kinds of small edits shouldn't hold us back from moving forward.

Please keep me posted once the final version goes out.

Best,
M. Glenn

Tip: click a sentence to mark it while you talk. Marks are only on your screen.

What to do · 6 minutes
  1. Minute 1 to 2. Each person: pick ONE sentence from the reply that has a hidden meaning. Post it like this: R2: "I trust you'll use your best judgment." Plain words: do not ask me again. Your own words, not a teammate's.
  2. Minute 3 to 5. Everyone: read each other's posts. Type agree, disagree, or add, with one reason. Pick the three strongest sentences together.
  3. Minute 6. The Typist posts R2 FINAL: the three sentences, each with the group's plain words meaning, and one line: how many of the four questions did Ms. Glenn answer?
One reading, not the only one

"Let's aim to make this round smoother than last time." → plain words: "I remember April. I want you to know I remember." It brings up the old mistake without naming it.

"I trust you'll use your best judgment with the info we have." → plain words: "Do not ask me again." And if a guess goes wrong, it was Sarah's judgment, not Ms. Glenn's silence.

"These kinds of small edits shouldn't hold us back." → plain words: "Your questions are small. You are slowing us down." A $180 reprint came from a "small edit."

"Please keep me posted once the final version goes out." → plain words: "Tell me after. Not before." She wants the result, not the decision.

And the four questions? Answered: zero.

Why this matters

Readers tend to read email as more negative than the writer meant, and the gap grows when the reader is lower in rank (Byron, 2008). So part of what Sarah hears may be hers. And four unanswered questions are not in her head. Both things are true at once. That is why you check the task before you judge the tone.

Round 3 · What do I still need? 5 minutes

Forget the tone for six minutes.

It is 11:45. The PDF is due at 3:00. Sarah has four open questions and zero answers. For each one, decide: what does Sarah do if nobody answers?

1RSVP link
Print the link now, or "RSVP at the Outreach Office"?
If wrong: a link that shows "coming soon" for two days, or parents with no way to RSVP online.
2Speaker title
Director or Dean?
If wrong: a dean's title printed wrong on 300 flyers with the college logo. Kevin in Communications will notice.
3Childcare hours
6:00 to 8:30, or 6:00 to 8:00?
If wrong: parents who planned on 8:30 find the childcare room closing at 8:00. The one mistake that lands on a child.
4Spanish version
Same print run, or online only?
If wrong: either a two day delay on everything, or Spanish speaking families get no printed flyer.
What to do · 5 minutes
  1. Minute 1. Split the four questions: the Typist takes question 1, the next person question 2, and so on. (Three people? One person takes two.)
  2. Minute 2 to 3. Each person posts about your question like this: R3: Q2 speaker title. If no answer by 2:00, Sarah uses the title on the college website. Risk if wrong: low. Use low, medium, or high.
  3. Minute 4 to 5. The Typist posts R3 FINAL: the four backup choices in a list, and which ONE question is too risky to guess, with the reason.
One way to sort them

Safe to decide on her own: the RSVP line can say "RSVP at the Outreach Office or online at ..." so it works either way. The Spanish version can go online now and print later, which costs nothing today.

Can be checked without Ms. Glenn: Dr. Reyes's title is on the college website and in Kevin's hands. The childcare hours are in the provider's contract.

The point: Sarah needs Ms. Glenn for far less than four things. Maybe for one. That changes what the next email has to do.

Round 4 · Choose the move 7 minutes

What does Sarah do at 11:50?

Every option below works in some situation. None of them is free. Pick the one that gets the flyer printed and keeps Sarah in good shape with her boss afterward.

A
Reply by email again.Shorter this time. Keeps a written record. Slow. Can be ignored again, and then it is 2:30.
B
Walk to her office, or call.Answers in two minutes if she is there. No record. She said she does not like being pulled into small things.
C
"I will go ahead unless I hear otherwise" email.Sarah writes what she will do on each item, and gives a time (2:00) after which she goes ahead. If something goes wrong later, the email shows she asked first. Sounds confident. Can also sound like "fine, I will do it my way."
D
Copy Kevin on the email.Communications approves the flyer anyway, and Kevin can confirm the title. Now a second person has seen the question. Can look like Sarah is going around her boss.
E
Go to Dr. Alvarez, the boss's boss.The Dean would answer. And Sarah, 8 months in, just went over her boss's head about a flyer.
F
Say nothing and guess.Fastest. This is what Sarah did in April. If it goes wrong, "I trust your judgment" becomes the record.

You can combine. B then C is a real strategy: try the door, and if she is not there, send the "proceeding unless" email. Say so if that is your group's answer.

What to do · 7 minutes
  1. Minute 1 to 2. Each person votes. Post one letter and one fact from the case file, like this: R4: C, because in April she never answered before the deadline either, so another email alone will not work. A feeling is not a reason. A fact from the case file is.
  2. Minute 3 to 6. Everyone: read the votes. If you disagree, type why, using the case. You may combine two moves (for example B then C). The group must agree on one answer.
  3. Minute 7. The Typist posts R4 FINAL: [the move] because [the reason]. Ruled out first: [a letter] because [why].
Round 5 · Draft it 9 minutes

Write the move.

Whatever your group chose, it has to be said. Build it together in the chat.

If your move is an email (A, C, or D)

Draft the whole email: subject line, greeting, body, closing. Keep it shorter than Sarah's first one.

  • It names the deadline: the print run is at 3:00, so it says by what time Sarah needs her answer.
  • It asks for the fewest things Sarah truly needs from Ms. Glenn. One is fine.
  • It says what Sarah will do on her own for anything she can decide herself.
  • It keeps the warmth. One line is enough.
  • It does not defend Sarah, mention April, or point out that nothing was answered.
  • It is something Ms. Glenn can answer with one word.
If your move is a call or a walk over (B)

Write the first two sentences Sarah says when Ms. Glenn looks up from her screen. Then write the three line email Sarah sends after the conversation so the answers exist in writing.

If your move is to go to the boss's boss, or to stay silent (E or F)

Write the one paragraph Sarah would say to Dr. Alvarez, or the dated note Sarah writes for herself at 3:00 recording what she decided and why. Then answer: what is the cost if this goes well? What is the cost if it does not?

What to do · 9 minutes
  1. Minute 1 to 3. The Typist writes a first version and posts it as R5 DRAFT: (the whole email, or the call opener plus the follow up email, depending on your Round 4 move).
  2. Minute 4 to 7. Each other person posts ONE change like this: R5 EDIT: cut "I appreciate your trust," it sounds like we are defending ourselves. Add, cut, or fix. Say why. Use the checklist above.
  3. Minute 8. The Typist posts the finished version as R5 FINAL:.
  4. Minute 9. Any one person reads the FINAL as if you were Ms. Glenn and posts R5 READER: what she hears, in one line.
To: Ms. Glenn  ·  Subject: Parent Night flyer: one yes or no by 2:00

Hi Ms. Glenn,

Thanks for the quick reply. I will handle the title, the childcare hours, and the Spanish version from the contract, the website, and Kevin, so you do not need to look at those.

One thing only you can call: print the RSVP link now, or print "RSVP at the Outreach Office"?

If I do not hear back by 2:00, I will print "RSVP at the Outreach Office or online," which works either way, and send the proof to Campus Print at 3:00.

Thank you,
Sarah

What it does: keeps the warmth, asks for one thing, says what Sarah will do on her own and by what time, puts the decision in writing, and never mentions April. If Ms. Glenn answers "link," great. If she says nothing, the flyer still prints and the record shows Sarah asked.

Round 6 · The long game 6 minutes

The flyer printed. It was fine.

The long game, plain words: not this flyer, the next six months with this boss. Thursday, 9:05 AM. Sarah sent the proof at 2:55 the day before. Ms. Glenn never answered the 2:00 question. This arrives.

From: Ms. Glenn  ·  Thursday 9:05 AM

Hi Sarah,

Thanks for the proof. Looks fine.

Next time, let's not need quite so many check ins before a print run.

M. Glenn

The question that is not about this flyer

This is the third time in eight months that Sarah asked for a decision in writing, got no decision, and then got a note about asking. The task is done. The pattern is not.

What to do · 6 minutes
  1. Minute 1 to 2. Each person posts R6: two things. Does Sarah reply to this email, yes or no, and why. And what Sarah writes down for herself today, in one line.
  2. Minute 3 to 5. Everyone: discuss by typing: who, if anyone, should Sarah talk to about this, and when: now, after the next time, or never?
  3. Minute 6. The Typist posts R6 FINAL: reply yes or no, who Sarah talks to and when, and two signs that would mean it is time to take this above her boss.
What tends to work

Do not reply to the tone. "Thanks, glad it worked. I will send decisions as a yes or no going forward so they take ten seconds." That answers the complaint by removing the thing she complained about.

Keep a dated note. One line per time: date, what was asked, what was answered, what happened. Not to build a case. To know whether this is three times or thirteen.

Going higher up is for a pattern that is costing something, not for one email. Plain words: escalate means to take the problem to someone above your boss. Signs it has become that: Sarah's name is attached to mistakes caused by missing decisions, the "check in" complaints show up in her review, or the pattern starts to cost the office money or parents a service.

The first conversation is still with Ms. Glenn, face to face, about the process, not about her tone: "Before print runs, could we do a five minute call the day before? Then I will not need to email you questions."
